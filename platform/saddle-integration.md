# Mesh Saddle Integration (PROPOSAL)

A functional saddle first — the flock has an active feather-pecking problem,
so the fabric earns its welfare cost by protecting the back — customizable
second. The tag must remain visible, inspectable and on its own harness.

## The saddle

- **Fabric**: breathable polyester athletic mesh ("cool mesh" class, as in the
  Hen Saver reference product), quick-drying, 60–71 °C machine washable.
  No canvas, no laminated or waterproof fabric — 42 °C summer peaks make
  non-breathable fabric a lethal risk, not a comfort issue.
- **Cut**: standard hen-saddle blank with wing elastic loops (seconds on/off,
  staff only). Bias-bound edges.
- **Colours in the flock**: muted, matte. The loud surface is the removable
  velcro badge patch (rear third), swappable without washing the saddle.
- **Sets**: ≥2 per hen, wear/wash rotation; wash after every wear block
  (60 °C+, detergent, high agitation); skin + parasite check at every change.
- **Removal SOP**: off above the vet-set temperature threshold; off during
  molt; off in persistent wet weather.

## Grommet window — how tag and saddle coexist

A **Ø38 mm** bound circular window in the fabric, positioned over the tag.
The tag rides its own figure-eight elastic through its own tabs, exactly as
today; the saddle only frames it. Ø38 clears:

- the bare tag (Ø31.94),
- the tag + keeper collar (OD 35.54),
- a fingertip lifting the fabric edge for the daily inspection.

The cap face — id, message, LED ring — stays readable through the window with
the saddle on.

## Fabric-lock ring (optional accessory, uses the bayonet)

Without registration the window can wander off the tag as the hen moves. The
fabric-lock ring fixes that using the platform itself:

1. The bound fabric edge drops over the collar's bayonet ring.
2. A **lock ring** (bayonet male, flat flange OD ~42, all edges ≥1 mm radius)
   twists on 60° and clamps the fabric annulus between its flange and the
   crown deck.

Result: the saddle is registered to the tag, cannot flap over the cap face,
and releases with the same quarter-turn as any accessory. The lock ring obeys
the full safety envelope in `accessories.md` (≤5 g, matte, bleach-safe).
With the lock ring mounted, the collar latch is covered — same forced service
order as every accessory.

The saddle works without the lock ring too (plain grommet, tag independent) —
that is the fallback if the bench tests find the clamped fabric transmits
snag loads the tabs shouldn't see.

## What this does NOT do

- No fabric ever passes between the tag's flat underside and the hen — the
  tag-to-skin interface stays exactly as engineered and welfare-checked.
- No stitching, hook or loop attaches to the tag or its elastic; the only
  contact point is the collar crown, via the standard bayonet.
- Not worn in heat, molt or wet per the SOP; the tag and its T1 accessory stay
  on year-round, so patron identity never disappears with the saddle.

## Before any hen wears one

Vet consult (pecking-protection cut, temperature threshold, molt timing);
certifier notification; sourcing trial of mesh blanks vs. sewing our own;
grommet + lock-ring fit test on a printed tag+collar; snag test (deliberate
pull on the fabric — must release or hold below the tab shear margin, decide
which after the bench numbers exist).
